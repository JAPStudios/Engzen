# Engzen: Funding and Production Plan

## Applicant and mission
Project: Engzen, a proposed free-to-use game-development and game-distribution ecosystem from Jelly & Peanut Studios (JAPStudios).

Core proposition: make game creation and distribution more accessible by bringing project creation, scripting, visual assets, testing, and publishing into a coherent workflow. The long-term vision includes a visual editor and modeling workflow, developer distribution, community features, and original games built with the tools.

The applicant's legal form, tax status, operating address, ownership of intellectual property, and eligibility for each funding route must be confirmed before any application is submitted. This document does not represent Engzen as an established tax-exempt nonprofit.

## Funding request strategy
Seek funding in stages. Initial requests should be tied to a prototype with defined deliverables and a short reporting period. Avoid asking one funder to finance every planned product and service.

### Stage 1 — Prototype
Target period: 6 months after funding and project kickoff.

Deliverables:
- documented product requirements and a public roadmap;
- a reproducible development build and setup instructions;
- a minimal project editor or workflow that can create, run, and package a small game;
- one small playable sample game;
- an asset import workflow and a documented route for adding 3D assets;
- automated smoke tests for the build and sample project;
- a demo video, issue tracker, and feedback form;
- a costed Stage 2 plan based on test results.

Acceptance evidence:
- tagged release or versioned build;
- build instructions that a new tester can follow;
- test results and known-limitations list;
- demo and user-feedback summary;
- itemized expense report.

### Stage 2 — Closed developer pilot
Invite a small group of external developers to test setup, editing, asset import, scripting, packaging, and distribution. Record failures, time-to-first-playable, hardware requirements, and accessibility issues. Do not promise large-scale hosting before load and security tests exist.

### Stage 3 — Public beta
Release only after core workflows, license terms, privacy disclosures, content moderation, incident response, backups, and service-cost controls are ready. Define what is free and what optional paid services, if any, could sustain operations.

## Proposed funding mix
1. Grants for eligible research, public-benefit, education, or creative production work.
2. New York digital-game production tax credits, if the specific production and applicant qualify. A tax credit is not the same as a grant or guaranteed upfront financing.
3. Crowdfunding for a clearly bounded prototype or original game, with realistic rewards and delivery terms.
4. Donations and sponsorships through an eligible recipient, with accurate statements about tax deductibility.
5. Paid production, modeling, software, or support contracts that can subsidize free core tools.
6. Strategic pilots with schools, libraries, creator communities, or educational organizations where there is a specific use case and partner commitment.

3. Select one prototype use case and freeze the initial scope.
4. Obtain contractor, software, hardware, insurance, hosting, and legal/accounting quotes as appropriate.
5. Replace the placeholder budget in `prototype-budget.csv` with actual costs and assumptions.
6. Check every opportunity against official current rules and record the date checked in `application-tracker.csv`.
7. Prepare a concise deck, demo, work plan, budget narrative, and evidence of user demand.
8. Submit only after confirming eligibility, deadlines, matching-fund requirements, and whether pre-award costs are excluded.

## Risks and mitigations
- Scope too broad: fund one demonstrable workflow first.
- Unverified technical claims: link claims to a tagged build and test evidence.
- Funding delay: stage work and do not incur costs dependent on an unawarded grant.
- Legal/tax mismatch: obtain professional advice before accepting restricted donations or describing contributions as tax-deductible.
- Online safety and operating costs: delay large-scale user-generated content until moderation, security, privacy, and support procedures are funded.
- Third-party IP: use original or properly licensed assets and avoid implying endorsement by named companies or game creators.

## Success measures
- number of independent testers completing setup;
- proportion of testers who create and package a playable sample;
- time from clean install to first playable build;
- number and severity of build-blocking issues;
- documented accessibility and hardware compatibility results;
- total prototype cost and cost per successful tester;
- qualified developer interest and partner commitments.
